# Frontier Is Not Fate — explanation for Jinhua

## The argument you are being asked to own

The motivating question is what a better model changes once people use it.
This edition answers a narrower question: what happened when particular workers
or machines received particular AI tools? The support and developer studies
compare access with no access. Neither isolates the marginal effect of upgrading
from a weaker model to a stronger one. TCV used a specialist reinforcement-learning
controller, a different intervention from a general-purpose chat assistant.

The positive argument is to follow the work far enough to see the useful result.
A support assistant can help agents resolve more problems. A developer can spend
longer producing an accepted change even if generating code becomes easier.
Engineers can train a controller that acts on physical plasma, while still doing
substantial work to define and evaluate useful performance. A frontier improvement
could change these mechanisms; measuring access alone cannot tell us how.

The contribution is your cross-case synthesis and inspectable explanation.
Productivity-complements research already studies complementary investment;
standard duration and capacity reasoning already distinguishes sums, maxima and
minimum station capacities. The Methods paragraph locates those debts using the
existing BIS and Ding records. This edition claims no new estimator. Differences
between these studies cannot establish that workflow design caused their different
results: tools, people, tasks, dates and estimation methods also differ.

The four scenes offer a short visible explanation and optional depth. The paper
shares their components and data. Thirty-one staged profiles and their original
histories remain outside the main reading path; none supplies a national score.
Your opening precedes Scene 1. The page is an argument a reader can
explore voluntarily, with substantial AI assistance recorded below.

## Five headline-claim cards

**1. Reported use includes different practices — observation.** Original: ECB
SAFE Q4 2025, Box 1, Chart A and annex. Measurement: weighted responses from
5,067 firms; 33% reported very infrequent/experimental use, 7% significant use.
Supported: a yes/no adoption count hides different reported practices. Excluded:
a productivity effect or a maturity ranking. Strongest objection: these are
subjective survey categories. The displayed categories sum to 98%; the unallocated
residual remains. Census breadth and Eurostat firm-size adoption use separate
populations and definitions. Occasional use or non-adoption may be a reasonable
choice; higher use is not itself the objective.

**2. One support deployment increased throughput — estimated empirical effect.**
Original: Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond, final QJE article, §V.A, Table II column 3;
§III.B for setting. Measurement: issues resolved/hour among 5,172 agents at an
anonymous firm and subcontractors. The published headline is approximately 15%
higher throughput with a GPT-3-based assistant, principally rolled out in fall
2020/winter 2021. Supported: an operational gain in this setting. Excluded: a
15% wage, welfare or national productivity gain. Strongest objection: rollout
could coincide with other changes that undermine the comparison trend.

The main design is staggered-rollout difference-in-differences, not a randomized
trial. Agent, calendar-month and tenure controls do not remove the need for a
credible counterfactual trend. Larger gains among less experienced/skilled agents,
adherence and learning evidence (§V.B; §VI.A–B) support a knowledge-transfer
account. Tool quality or other workplace changes remain relevant alternatives.
The normalized visual sets a reference at 100 and shows 115: a transformation of
the reported relative estimate, not raw observed group means. No percentage
confidence interval has been invented. Small quality declines among the most
skilled agents complicate the average gain.

**3. An early developer experiment found longer task times — estimated empirical
effect.** Original: METR paper v2, §§2.1–2.3, Figure 1, Appendix D, read with the
24 February 2026 update. Measurement: 246 tasks, 16 experienced developers working
in familiar repositories, primarily using Cursor with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet in
early 2025. AI access was randomized by task. Supported: 19% longer completion
times in that selected setting, 95% interval +2% to +39%. Excluded: a current-model
or software-sector verdict. Strongest objection: this population and task set
transfer poorly to other development work.

The chart transforms that estimate into 100 versus 119, with interval 102–139;
it does not create a common metric with support throughput. Activity recordings
and participant accounts (§§2.4, 3.2–3.3; Figure 6; Appendix C) suggest prompting,
checking and waiting as possible mechanisms, not a causal decomposition. Forecasts
were elicited before assignment; they were not “preregistered difficulty forecasts.”
The February update reports selection by people and tasks expected to benefit,
preventing a reliable later estimate. That limitation stays beside the result.

**4. A learned controller operated physical fusion hardware — demonstrated result.**
Original: Degrave et al., Nature, Figure 1, Discussion, Methods: Deployment,
Extended Data Figure 4b. Measurement: plasma configurations controlled on TCV
with a simulation-trained policy. Supported: AI operated a physical feedback
loop; engineers supplied objectives, simulation and deployment infrastructure.
Excluded: a general chat-model upgrade effect or an AI-caused plant schedule
improvement. Strongest objection: transfer beyond the tested machine and
conditions requires its own evidence.

The original diagram separates simulation training, fixed-policy physical
control and post-experiment reconstruction for evaluation. Reconstruction is
not an online observer inside the policy. Extended Data Figure 4b describes
an initially omitted balancing objective and an alternative coil combination
that produced forces on machine structures; a later experiment added balancing.
This supports scrutiny of objective specification. It does not establish an
accident, dangerous loss of control or existential risk. Materials qualification
and plant availability sit alongside control as other dependencies, not its
consequences. Planned exposure is not an accepted qualification dataset.

**5. The location of a model lab does not allocate the gain — interpretation.**
Original: QJE Introduction, §III.B, Table I and §VII. The actor map distinguishes
OpenAI, an unnamed assistant supplier, the anonymous employer/subcontractors,
workers mainly in the Philippines and US small-business customers. Supported:
production crosses organizational and national boundaries. Excluded: national
shares of profits, taxes, wages, welfare or strategic leverage. Strongest objection:
the map locates roles without measuring value capture. Its equal-width arrows
encode relationships, not financial flows. Table I's 89% outside the US is not
an 89% Philippine share. Country comparison needs deployment scale, access terms,
worker outcomes and benefit capture beyond the model developer's address.

## Explain the actual interaction aloud

All inputs are invented. Baseline design takes eight days and testing 32.
Serial completion is 8 + 32 = 40 days. Fourfold design speed gives 8/4 + 32/1
= 34 days. That saves six days, or 15% of baseline time. It demonstrates conditional
arithmetic; no empirical bottleneck has been identified.

One optional input adds integration, review or rework after the required tasks.
It is incremental to work already included in the baseline and defaults to zero.
With six added days, completion is 2 + 32 + 6 = 40: unchanged. With eight, it is
42: two days or 5% MORE time. Quality is held constant by assumption. The input
is not calibrated from METR and cannot establish what real checking costs.

Parallel mode assumes independent tasks, both required. Baseline is max(8,32)
= 32; changed completion is max(2,32) plus added time. This is a different topology,
not permission to test a design before it exists. If testing also becomes four
times as fast and added time is zero, serial completion is 10 days and parallel
completion eight. The model permits large gains as well as losses.

The independent station example assumes capacities of 10, 4 and 6 accepted
units/week. Steady output cannot exceed min(10,4,6) = 4. Raising the first station
fourfold gives min(40,4,6) = 4. Raising the second fourfold gives min(40,16,6) = 6:
station three now binds. The visual marks that station and keeps a fixed 0–160
scale. Project overhead never enters this independent model. Compatible units,
adequate demand and buffers, constant quality, and no variability, losses or
startup delay are simplifying assumptions. Neither example estimates a financial
return, a fusion date or national capacity.

## Eight skeptical questions

1. **What is new?** The cross-case explanation and attribution questions, not a new estimator.
2. **Is conversion circular?** It would be if inferred from outcomes and then used to explain them. Future tests need independently specified conditions.
3. **Does this answer the upgrade question?** It frames it; these access studies do not estimate it.
4. **Why these cases?** Finite, readable contrasts, not a representative or systematic sample.
5. **Did workflow cause the difference?** Unresolved; tools, participants and estimators also differ.
6. **Could AI change a physical stage?** TCV shows one such intervention; wider effects require their own tests.
7. **Who captures the gain?** Mostly unmeasured here; operational output cannot substitute for welfare or power.
8. **What must I understand myself?** The comparison behind each headline, the toy arithmetic, and why your interpretation survives its strongest alternative.
