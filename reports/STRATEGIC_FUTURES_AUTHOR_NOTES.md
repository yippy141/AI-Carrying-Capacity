## What the product now argues

The flagship asks when advanced AI becomes durable national power, and what that
power does for people. The opening gives the frontier-compounding case strong
conditions: an initial US-based coalition leads in relevant capability, retains
exclusive access long enough to exploit it, repeatedly improves the process of
making AI, and uses AI-assisted engineering to change physical deployment. This
is a chosen future to examine, not a measurement of the present US–China balance.

That case deserves serious attention. A tool that improves the process of making
its successor may change the production of advantage itself. If the resulting
research tools remain exclusive, their owners could influence access terms,
research priorities and which public institutions receive useful systems first.
Those are plausible routes to bargaining power. They do not require pretending
that a laboratory owns every supplier or that geopolitical strength benefits
every household.

The alternatives ask which part of that chain fails, changes hands or changes
purpose. Useful capability might reach rivals before the leader consolidates
power. Physical deployment might keep its existing route, or AI might enable a
validated redesign. Productive gains might spread between firms while ownership
remains concentrated. A credible international bargain might redirect research
and deployment toward agreed missions. Conflict or lost effective control could
interrupt all the orderly trajectories.

The contribution is the cross-case strategic synthesis and an inspectable set of
conditional rules. It is not a new estimator, a national index or a calibrated
simulation. The earlier adoption and productivity study remains at `/work`, with
its paper at `/work/paper`. Those studies establish bounded operational results;
they cannot settle the international future by themselves.

## Every implemented rule, in ordinary language

**Starting premise and horizon.** The initial lead belongs to a hypothetical
US-based frontier coalition. Chinese developers retain their own research; other
countries have firms, institutions and specialized suppliers with agency. The
2026 opening, “before 2030,” “the 2030s” and “toward 2040” are narrative windows,
not computed arrival dates. No current national rank or future probability is
stored. The external scenario crosswalk and eight accepted coupling dispositions
remain separate records.

**1. Research feedback.** “Repeated AI-assisted advances” assumes that gains recur
across successive research cycles, are evaluated reliably, and are reinvested.
A return path becomes active in the diagram. “Bounded task improvements” keeps
useful research assistance but removes the assumed reinforcing loop. No annual
rate, benchmark multiplier or doubling time is computed. This distinction
matters because an isolated saving and a process that reliably produces more
savings have different strategic implications.

**2. Diffusion ordering.** Choose whether relevant usable capability reaches rivals
before or after the initial lead consolidates. Earlier arrival changes Chinese
access and third-country supplier choice, and weakens the exclusive-access route
to leverage. It does not hand over hardware, staff, the complete training stack,
ownership or every remaining frontier capability. Later arrival preserves an
option to exploit exclusivity; it does not prove successful coercion or rent
capture. These are selected relative timescales, not measured diffusion lags.

**3. The physical production path.** “Validated process redesign” assumes that AI-
assisted engineering changes integration, laboratory operations or equipment
processes and that equipment, qualified staff and validation are actually
mobilized. The diagram includes design-to-deployment and deployment-to-design
connections. Existing-path mode removes that newly assumed loop while retaining
qualification and integration tasks. Redesign can be selected even with bounded
research feedback: useful engineering advances do not logically require recursive
AI improvement. The model therefore does not force physical constraints to win.
Neither setting estimates how much capital or time a country needs.

**4. Investment and the political bargain.** This is deliberately one bundled
choice. Research-first control prioritizes frontier experiments, then selected
deployments, with broad access and assurance deferred at the margin. The broad-
benefit choice gives deployment/public access first call, then assurance and
worker adjustment, while postponing some frontier work. A verified compact
prioritizes verification and agreed missions, allows selected deployment, and
restricts unrestricted experimentation. With restricted diffusion, it represents
a limited-access agreement, not universal sharing. Interruption prioritizes
continuity/control recovery and essential services and withdraws expansion claims.

Each ordering applies to a finite discretionary agenda. It creates no new stock
of resources. Money, chips, power, skilled people and institutional attention
are not interchangeable, and the prototype does not assert that any actual
national budget is feasible. The broad-benefit branch stipulates procurement,
access and adjustment channels; it does not establish they succeed. Ownership,
procurement and investment could vary independently in reality. Their bundling
is the main simplification to remember when explaining this control.

## Work through the actual comparison

Start at the default “The lead compounds.” The research loop and new deployment
route are active. The rule output says a protected research lead can compound;
it also says output can grow while benefits concentrate.

Change diffusion to “Before power consolidates.” Chinese developers now have
access to relevant usable capability, third-country firms have more supplier
choice, and exclusive leverage weakens. Household benefit has not changed:
diffusion is not redistribution. Next change deployment to “Improve within the
existing path.” The new design/operations loop becomes inactive and qualification
and integration again appear as separate requirements. The before/after table
retains the original possibility alongside the changed one.

Pin that case as “before,” then select broad-benefit investment. The benefit
channel changes and frontier experiments move down the priority ordering. No
percentage increase in national welfare appears. Reset returns both the selected
and pinned cases to the original default. Four preset buttons set the same four
inputs; they are proposals, not mutually exclusive or exhaustive probabilities.

The canvas’s positions and arrow widths stay fixed. Outlines mark changed nodes;
dashed lines mark changeable scenario feedback, and faded lines are inactive or
unassessed. Both national actor groups research and deploy. Text labels and a
comparison table carry the interpretation without relying on color. The default
is server-rendered and readable without JavaScript. Checkpoints change with the
selected premises: an observation supporting early diffusion weakens the
protected-access alternative. Interruption needs restored continuity before the
ordinary research/diffusion tests become applicable again.

## The source readings that matter most

1. **AI can improve part of AI production.** Google’s originating AlphaEvolve
   announcement reports a kernel improvement that reduced total Gemini training
   time by 1%. Read “Enhancing AI training and inference.” This is a first-party,
   bounded report. The much larger kernel speedup is not the whole-training gain,
   and neither supplies recurring growth. The leap from this example to reliable
   repeated research feedback is the prototype’s strongest unestablished premise.
2. **Released weights supply a diffusion channel.** DeepSeek’s original R1 report
   and README record weights and six distilled models. Read the original report
   and README §§1–3 and 7. Access does not establish cheap serving, reliability or
   reproduced training. Original v1 is dated; later v2 is acknowledged. Relevant
   Qwen/Llama upstream terms are not erased by the repository’s MIT statement.
3. **Both national programmes address models and deployment.** Compare China’s
   AI+ opinion, II（二） and III（七/十）, with the US Action Plan’s printed pp. 4–5,
   14–16 and 20. These are programme intentions, not delivered results. The
   Chinese summary is AI-assisted; native-language human review is pending.
4. **Specialized supply can matter without becoming a permanent ceiling.** ASML’s
   2025 report, printed p. 69, describes dependencies and manufacturing locations.
   IEA’s 2025 executive summary discusses grid/equipment constraints and possible
   mitigations. Neither gives this prototype a national growth coefficient,
   disruption probability or demonstrated 2040 outcome.

AI 2027 supplies a forecast-bearing rapid-compounding account; its 2025 story is
not an observation of what has happened in 2026. Plan A recommends a regime and
makes conditional predictions about it, rather than stating an unconditional
best guess. AI as Normal Technology distinguishes invention, applications and
diffusion in an argued predictive and normative account. None becomes Jinhua’s
forecast by appearing here. Retrieved scenario-page versions are fingerprinted;
full third-party texts and graphics are not redistributed.

Conversations in Washington and my experience at the World AI Conference in

**Interpretation — `author.judgment`; home, paper and About:**

I want to take the compounding case seriously. If AI improves the research that
produces its successors—and helps redesign the machinery around it—a lead could
become much more than an advantage in chat software. The difficult question is
whether control over that process stays exclusive long enough to become durable
power.

**Purpose — `author.closing`; home, paper and About:**

I care about who gets to decide what this power is for. A state could become
harder to coerce while many of its citizens gain little. A society could become
healthier or more capable without owning the leading laboratory. I want the
comparison to keep both possibilities in view.
