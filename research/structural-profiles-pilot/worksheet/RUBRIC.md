# S1-S5 coding rubric

Use this rubric only for the frozen stage scope shown in the submission
workbook. Higher always means structurally easier for AI-driven improvement.
The five dimensions remain separate: never sum, average, weight, rank, or
percentage-transform S1-S5.

The `0_means` and `4_means` columns are the canonical endpoints from the
method. The `2_guidance` column is operational V1 coding guidance, not a
cardinal midpoint and not a new method rule. A value of 1 or 3 records an
intermediate judgment and requires an explicit rationale; it must not be
produced by mechanical interpolation.

| Dimension | Name | 0_means | 2_guidance | 4_means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | Information intensity of the scoped stage | Progress occurs primarily through physical transformation, biological processes, construction, or accumulated operating time. | Stage progress materially combines informational and physical, biological, or operational work; neither clearly dominates under the stated scope. | Progress occurs primarily through information processing, analysis, design, inference, communication, or software. |
| S2 | Feedback speed | Cycles take years to decades. | The ordinary learn-test-revise cycle is generally measured in weeks to months, rather than minutes/days or years/decades. | Learn-test-revise cycles occur in minutes to days. |
| S3 | Experiment affordability and throughput | Attempts are scarce and expensive. | Attempts require meaningful resources or scarce systems but remain repeatable; throughput or parallelism is material but constrained. | Attempts are near-free and parallel. |
| S4 | Physical flexibility | Clock time is dominated by physical floors. | Physical processes create meaningful delay, but no single physical, biological, construction, curing, healing, or qualification floor dominates the entire scoped stage. | No construction, growth, curing, healing, or qualification floor dominates elapsed time. |
| S5 | Intrinsic error tolerance | Errors are catastrophic or irreversible. | Errors create material cost, delay, or review burdens, but are usually containable and reversible within the stated scope. | Errors are cheap, reversible, and low-externality. |

## Coding reminders

- S1 describes how progress occurs inside the scoped stage, not the stage's
  share of a wider schedule. `critical_path_role` remains separate.
- S2 uses the ordinary scoped feedback loop, not an ideal demonstration.
- Every S3 rationale must address both marginal cost per attempt and attainable
  parallel or serial throughput.
- S4 concerns intrinsic physical time floors inside the stated scope.
- S5 concerns consequences and reversibility inside the stated scope.
- Missing evidence or an unresolved judgment stays missing; never invent a
  source ID or force a value.
