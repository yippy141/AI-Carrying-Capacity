## The central Atlas thesis (claim ledger clm-0029)

Frontier capability converts fastest where work is digital and feedback-rich (AI R&D, coding, cyber) and stalls where output depends on hardware, regulation, procurement, and trust (manufacturing, healthcare, public services). Status: approved_with_caveat — ordinal judgment; Figure 2 placements are illustrative, not scored.

## Standing falsifiers

- Direct microdata showing no relationship between actual AI workflow use and firm productivity even in high-readiness sectors (kills H1/H3).
- Uniform returns across sectors, regions, firms once measured properly (kills the heterogeneity mechanism).
- Sustained low utilization of compute and smart-manufacturing assets **with** compensating output/profit/export gains (weakens H8 in one direction) — or without them (shifts the story to overbuild).
- Open-weight models compressing the useful frontier gap until frontier sensitivity collapses as an axis (reframes the whole x-axis; clm-0030).
- Credible public-sector evaluations showing durable, controlled service-quality gains at scale (upgrades H7 from lag to led).

## Do-not-claim list (from the staged evidence review)

- Do not claim AI has produced broad national productivity acceleration outside tightly measured contexts.
- Do not claim frontier-model-owning countries have higher near-term returns than API/open-weight-using countries.
- Do not claim public-sector pilots prove state-capacity gains.
- Do not attribute current layoffs or hiring weakness to AI alone.
- Do not treat announced investment packages as observed buildout.
