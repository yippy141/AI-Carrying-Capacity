# Research Question

Date: 2026-07-11

## Core question

**How does the marginal national return to accessible frontier AI capability vary by country, sector, conversion capacity, and outcome type?**

In plain terms: what does an additional increment of frontier AI capability actually buy a country — and why does the same increment buy different things in different places?

## Decomposition

The question separates four analytically distinct objects (per the 2026-07-11 evidence review):

1. **Frontier capability** — what the best available systems can do at a given reliability, cost, and task duration. Observed in benchmark suites, price-performance data, and time-horizon evaluations, not national statistics.
2. **Accessible capability** — what actors in a country can actually use, given export controls, pricing, cloud access, language support, and open-weight availability. This is where "sovereignty" matters empirically.
3. **Conversion capacity** — the national and sectoral stock of complements: power, data centers, firm capabilities, management, finance, skills, trust, procurement, regulatory clarity.
4. **Outcomes, split by stage** — **build** (capex, infrastructure), **use** (adoption and intensity), **harvest** (productivity, quality, innovation), **distribute** (wages, employment, welfare, strategic capacity).

## Why the stage split is the method

The evidence base is structurally unbalanced: strong at build (energy, capex data), improving at use (adoption surveys), thin at harvest (task-level RCTs only), and early at distribute. Pooling stages produces overclaiming; splitting them produces the paper's actual finding — build is visible, harvest is not yet, and the gap between them is the conversion problem.

## What this question is not

- Not "who is winning the AI race" — no composite score, no ranking.
- Not a causal macro estimation — no identification strategy exists for national GDP effects of frontier capability, and the product must not imply one.
- Not a forecastable single number — the forecast register tracks falsifiable signposts, not a headline prediction.

## Value tagging

Every value in the product is tagged as one of: **observed data**, **model estimate**, **official target**, **survey response**, or **scenario/commentary**. Investment announcements (e.g., France €109B, EU InvestAI €200B, Canada strategy targets) are official targets and are never mixed with realized outcomes.
